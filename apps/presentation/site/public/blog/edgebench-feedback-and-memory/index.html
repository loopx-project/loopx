Giving long-horizon agents a reason for their next step

From reliable feedback to sustained progress and affordable context

The useful question after a long agent run is what improved after the first good result, whether the improvement survived, and what the entire process cost.

Our EdgeBench research has moved through three stages: make evaluation and continuation reliable; examine how accumulated evidence changes later work; then reduce the cost of delivering that evidence. Early Lean results and later Ad Placement traces show real progress. The strongest foundation-model baselines have advanced too. These observations support continued development of LoopX, while leaving the contribution and net benefit of individual mechanisms open.

Our current judgment is encouraging but bounded. We have examples of useful late work and a more direct recovery/replanning flow. We do not yet have a general causal improvement estimate or a complete fee comparison.

1. What should get better with more time?

LoopX keeps the original goal, acceptance conditions, work ownership and evidence durable across sessions and hosts. Its control plane constrains state transitions and supplies feedback for the model’s next choice. It does not certify that the choice is insightful. A recorded finding, a read receipt and a replan entry are intermediate evidence; the relevant chain ends in actual work and a validated outcome.

  1. GoalAcceptance and scope
  2. EvidenceResults and limitations
  3. DecisionContinue or change course
  4. WorkExecute and validate
  5. RetentionKeep the best valid result

We separate three observations: an initial advantage, an improvement obtained later, and retention of the best validated artifact. A higher final score can come almost entirely from a stronger starting point. A better intermediate candidate can disappear from the terminal workspace. More runtime, Todos or graph nodes establish none of these outcomes.

Comparison boundaries

Historical studies include Official, single-call, native Goal, LoopX Resume and LoopX Explore. Explore adds optional evidence capture and planning to continued work. Blind runs retain public tasks, training data and local validators but receive no hidden judge scores or diagnostics; background operator evaluation is not solver feedback. Later native-feedback studies use agent-requested evaluation, a one-hour accepted-submission cooldown and no periodic grading. These are different studies, not one pooled treatment.

Models, effort, prompts, runners and budgets co-evolved. Early studies primarily used Sol/xhigh; later Sol/max and Astra/xhigh cohorts retain their own conditions. One run per arm is descriptive evidence. Missing, stopped, invalid and valid-zero results stay distinct. Source time, evaluation opportunities and cooldown survive checkpoint continuation; donor history is not a new independent repetition.

2. First make feedback and continuation trustworthy

The first Portfolio experiments exposed measurement and execution defects before they could tell us much about strategy. A blind wrapper had removed iterative optimization guidance along with the judge entrypoint. A ledger check attributed earlier market returns to holdings bought only at the closing price. Both confounded the intended comparison; repairing them required new attempts while preserving the old results.

One small oracle made the accounting error clear. Start with 100,000 cash, buy 100 units at a closing price of 200, and pay 10 commission. Immediate equity is 99,990 and NAV is 0.9999; the new holding did not earn the preceding rise from 100. Independent reconciliation then matched all 243 days in each of two frozen samples, with equity residual below 4×10⁻⁹ and no share-count difference. This established ledger consistency, not investment skill. The versioned repair is documented in EdgeBench #14.

Continuation had a related failure: an outer process could wake repeatedly after a work item ended without recovering a useful next action. The governing question must return to the original goal. Completing a Todo can justify another experiment, waiting, or completion; it cannot by itself settle goal acceptance. Correct recovery and feedback make subsequent research interpretable.

3. Look beyond the first good result

Later experiments shifted the emphasis to work after many hours. We compare a common observation window, then inspect the initial result, later increments and retained candidate. A final ranking alone hides these differences.

Ad Placement provides a concrete instance of later progress rather than a universal plateau. A worse subsequent candidate was rejected and the best version survived. Its raw-score lead is modest—about 0.052%—and only that arm has the cited terminal-workspace verification. The old/new packages changed core and runner together, so this is not an isolated LoopX-feature effect.

The Astra result expands the foundation-model frontier in our observations. Official separated speed changes from search changes and obtained successive native improvements. That progress belongs to the foundation/Official path; it cannot be credited to LoopX. Stronger models also raise the standard for a useful harness: context and review costs must earn their place against an improving baseline.

Schema: an initial lead is not sustained improvement

Schema is the detailed counterexample. In the Sol old/new 14-hour window, the newer run scored 0.804620 versus 0.723684. It already led at the first native result, 0.794620 versus 0.713158, received at different elapsed times. Subsequent increments were only 0.010000 and 0.010526. The larger endpoint advantage mostly reflected the initial result; continuing code changes did not close the six original target requirements.

A separate Astra/xhigh 12-hour comparison was less favorable: Official reached 0.881813 and LoopX 0.786257, with neither improving its best between 6h and 12h. LoopX recorded 36 completed compactions versus four, and about 5.32 times the uncached input. Compaction can overlap useful work, and versions, recovery, shared load and scorer conditions differ. The trace still identifies the practical challenge: producing more changes and more control activity did not produce more accepted value.

Conditions and limits of these windows

The Sol old/new study used planned + Explore, Sol/xhigh, TurnEnvelope off, agent-requested native feedback and a one-hour cooldown. The 14-hour window was selected after earlier observations as the largest whole hour below the earliest actual termination; it was not an 18-hour completion. The separate Astra study compares each arm’s own first 12 outer hours, including preparation. The Sol window reconciles 7,522 unique responses and 52 received native results; Astra reconciles 5,277 responses and 45 results. Cumulative counters were not repeatedly added; reasoning is included in output. Historical infrastructure failures and unknown whole-run integrity remain recorded. Different treatments, adaptive restarts and a prior operator exposure to an Official terminal aggregate prevent a clean causal verdict.

4. Ask whether evidence changed the next work

A mature checkpoint helps inspect a specific decision boundary. Both receivers inherit the same scientific state and incumbent; we then follow the evidence they read, the experiment they choose, the work they actually perform and the native feedback. A negative local result can be useful if it rules out a branch under stated conditions. Replanning need not force a direction change when continuing is justified.

Recent work on #7269 makes the reader first consume the goal, facts and feedback, then the writeback format. It also releases obsolete task-selection preference and preserves the original Turn settlement after a successor is created. This addresses two observed sources of wasted interaction: reading writer instructions before deciding, and needing another round to resolve which work was settled.

This is limited positive evidence for the flow, with a small Grid outcome advantage and neutral Schema feedback. It does not establish whole-task acceleration. The study used one pair per case, the same original checkpoint per pair, Sol/max, normal cadence three effective work Turns, and context differencing, TurnEnvelope and stride off. Preparation temporarily used cadence one until the restored semantic review and native Turn completed. It is not a study of repeated steady-state three-Turn reviews.

Earlier retained/fresh-context checkpoint work on Graph and Portfolio asked a different question. Its source age, reconstruction charge and continuation phase stay within the original cumulative 36-hour ceiling. An earlier nominal “with #7197” arm never received the staged reader, so its score cannot establish that treatment’s benefit or harm. A later Astra-to-Sol continuation changes model and effort too. These histories remain useful diagnostics, but are not independent cold runs or interchangeable ablations.

5. Efficiency means the complete episode

The full bill includes model input and output, tool responses and retries, context reconstruction, review and settlement, evaluation, shared resources and operator intervention. Cached input is cheaper than uncached input under some billing arrangements, but it is not free. Reasoning tokens are already part of output. Without actual pricing/account reconciliation, a token vector is evidence about usage rather than a fee estimate.

Reduce repeated delivery without losing obligations

Staged reading and context differencing solve related problems. Staging orders decision evidence before writer instructions. Differencing sends changes against an exact baseline while keeping current controls explicit. Within the same native session and context epoch, the Hook path uses an explicit engineering assumption of continuity after a complete paged read: normally delivered tool content remains usable. It no longer requires full model-input diagnostics during ordinary operation, and a read receipt is not relabeled as independent retention proof. Session changes, observed compaction, incomplete reads and known delivery failures invalidate the baseline; recovery uses the same immutable capture. Missed hooks and unseen downstream truncation can still escape detection and remain validation limits.

Historical offline replay illustrates why totals matter: structured Host and native presentation bytes fell 57.72% and 60.23%, while native entry plus full recovery rose 6.37%. These are historical byte observations, not current-head model-token results. An earlier offline probe also reported a 2.25% increase in CLI bytes, but its qualification as an actual CLI delivery measurement was withdrawn: it omitted final CLI compaction and reformatted entry output. That figure remains only as an invalid historical measurement and supports no complete-delivery or cost conclusion. A 60% smaller view cannot be reported as 60% lower total cost.

Our current efficiency work connects ordinary and staged delivery to the shared transport, reduces repeated reads and rebinding, and removes avoidable settlement round trips. The default with differencing off matters as much as the opt-in path. Enabled delivery also has to recover correctly and remain easy to activate across the product and benchmark. The reference of 15% for all mechanisms combined remains unqualified; it is neither a separate allowance per mechanism nor a reason to omit failures or reconstruction.

6. Context validation starts from zero

The current context study starts Grid, Schema and Ad Placement from their original tasks with Sol/max and an 18-hour upper limit per case. Historical Official or older LoopX runs provide descriptive references where usable. Their model/effort, source, scorer, opportunity and clock differences remain visible; they are not strict matched controls. A new baseline is warranted where the old material cannot answer the relevant question. Attempts with different source versions, continuity bases or transports are analyzed separately, rather than pooled as one treatment.

First verify that actual delivery changes: the initial full read, a subsequent delta with its continuity basis stated, complete fallback after known baseline loss, and a full review-to-work interaction. Then compare useful progress, retained artifacts and the complete usage vector. Requested flags, receipts and smaller local packets cannot substitute for that evidence. If the feature fails its intended behavior, stop the affected attempt, preserve its costs and history, repair it and use a new attempt identity.

The first Grid and Ad interactions on the new native Hook path have completed full paging, one delta and subsequent task work. Within each same capture, the delta payload was 19.53% and 19.48% smaller than its corresponding full packet. These are single-read payload comparisons: they exclude page envelopes, subsequent model input, hooks, recovery and review costs. They do not establish lower total cost. A complete review-to-native-outcome cycle, sustained benefit and failure recovery remain to be verified.

Our effectiveness target is useful progress after many hours and reliable retention of it. Our efficiency target is comparable accepted value with less complete cost and human attention. The next result must address both. Current runs retain the original deadline, and failed or replaced attempts retain their costs and history.
